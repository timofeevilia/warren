01 — The wind

It starts
at the Sun

The Sun leaks. A thin stream of charged particles blows outward in every direction, all the time. A spacecraft parked 1.5 million km upstream of Earth measures it before it arrives.

02 — The door

A shield
with a latch

Earth's magnetic field deflects most of that wind. What decides whether any gets in is one number: Bz, the north–south tilt of the magnetic field the wind carries. North, and the door stays shut. South, and the two fields connect.

— nT waiting for data

03 — The oval

A ring
of light

Particles that get in spiral down the field lines and hit the upper atmosphere in a ring around each magnetic pole. Oxygen glows green at 100 km, violet higher up. The stronger the storm, the wider the ring, measured by the Kp index from 0 to 9.

04 — The mirror

The other
pole

The same field lines come down in the south, so every aurora has a twin. The southern lights are just as bright; almost nobody lives under them. Most of this ring hangs over open ocean and Antarctic ice.
